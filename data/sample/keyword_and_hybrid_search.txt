Keyword search, such as BM25, scores documents by how often query terms appear in them, weighted by how rare each term is across the collection and normalised by document length. BM25 is excellent when the question contains exact identifiers, names or technical terms, but it cannot match synonyms. Hybrid retrieval runs keyword search and dense vector search in parallel and merges the two ranked lists. A popular merging method is Reciprocal Rank Fusion, which gives each document a score of one divided by a constant plus its rank in each list, then sums the scores. The constant is commonly set to sixty. After fusion, a reranker such as a cross-encoder reads the question together with each candidate passage and produces a more accurate relevance score, so only the best few passages are sent to the language model. Reranking costs extra latency but often improves answer quality noticeably.
