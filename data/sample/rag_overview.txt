Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, combines a retriever with a large language model. Instead of relying only on what the model memorised during training, the system first searches an external knowledge base and then passes the most relevant passages to the model as context. This reduces hallucinations, makes answers traceable to sources, and allows knowledge to be updated by changing documents rather than retraining the model. A typical RAG pipeline has an offline indexing stage and an online query stage. During indexing, documents are loaded, cleaned, split into chunks, converted to embedding vectors and stored in an index. During querying, the user question is embedded, the nearest chunks are retrieved, and a prompt containing the question and the chunks is sent to the language model which writes a grounded answer. Chunk size and overlap are important design choices: chunks that are too small lose context, while chunks that are too large add noise and cost more tokens.
