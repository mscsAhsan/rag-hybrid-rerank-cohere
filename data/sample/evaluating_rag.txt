Evaluating a RAG system means checking both the retriever and the generator. For retrieval, common metrics are recall at k, which asks whether the correct passage appears in the top k results, and mean reciprocal rank, which rewards putting the correct passage near the top. For generation, teams check faithfulness, meaning the answer is supported by the retrieved context, and answer relevance, meaning it actually addresses the question. A small hand-written test set of questions with known source passages is often enough to compare chunk sizes, embedding models or retrieval strategies. Common failure modes include missing the right chunk because of poor chunking, retrieving near duplicates that waste the context window, and a model ignoring the context and answering from memory. Instructing the model to cite passages and to admit when the answer is not in the context helps reduce these problems.
